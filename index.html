Vilmantas Marcinkevičius

Vilmantas Marcinkevičius is one of the most prominent contemporary neo-expressionist painters in Northern Europe. His painting Tired Man is part of the European Parliament’s art collection, acquired in 2008, and his work is also held by the MO Museum and the Lithuanian National Museum of Art, the TAAD Foundation and a museum collection in Hiroshima, Japan. He painted portraits for the Danish royal family. Viborg, Denmark, named him Artist of the Year in 2000. He has held 30 solo exhibitions across Lithuania, Denmark, France, Sweden and the Faroe Islands. For nearly two decades he has worked with Galleri NB, one of Denmark’s strongest galleries, and in 2021 his paintings were presented in the United Arab Emirates and South Africa.

As co-founder of the Young Painter Prize, he helps choose winners whose work enters Lithuania’s national museum collection. The prize now brings finalists together in international exhibitions. In 2026 the Museo Civico Pietro Cavoti in Galatina, Italy, presented First, We Bite: Icons, Offerings and the Feminine in Ritual, his joint exhibition with Indra Marcinkevičienė, curated by Neringa J. Miller. He divides his time between Lithuania and Galatina, in southern Italy.

His work is now introduced to American collectors through SAVAS Art House.

Indra Marcinkevičienė

Indra Marcinkevičienė (born 1969, Vilnius) is a Lithuanian artist and designer who creates opulent, maximalist interiors: velvet furniture, sculptural textile wall works and carpets in saturated colour, with a strong sense of theatre and wit. She trained in theatre make-up design in Vilnius and spent a decade as a stylist for leading fashion magazines before turning to interiors, furniture and art. Her motto, It’s never enough, sums up her approach.

Her furniture has been shown at Maison & Objet in Paris, London Design Week, the Philadelphia Museum of Art Craft Show, IFFS Singapore, Interior Lifestyle Tokyo and the Collect fair at London’s Saatchi Gallery, where she presented Happy Boomerang in 2015. Her chair Adam won Lithuania’s Good Design award in 2012, and New Adam was nominated for the iF Design Award in 2014. She received an award for her stand at the Philadelphia Museum of Art Craft Show in 2013, the jury award at Craft Vilnius in 2014 and a prize at the Salon d’Arts Plastiques in La Rochelle in 2020. Her interiors have won Lithuanian design awards and appeared in magazines across Europe and in Andrew Martin’s Interior Design Review.

As an artist she explores the emotional world of women, war and life under political oppression, from Madonnas to ironic, banana-like fountain sculptures. She has exhibited at the Round Tower in Copenhagen, Galleri NB in Denmark, the M. K. Čiurlionis National Museum of Art in Kaunas, the Grand Théâtre de Provence in Aix-en-Provence and in Metz, at Louise Gallery in Durbuy, Belgium, and in Weimar, Germany. In 2024 she also showed Gentle Resistance with the photographer Antanas Sutkus in Bourganeuf, France, and exhibited at the Art Vilnius’24 contemporary art fair. In 2026 the Museo Civico Pietro Cavoti in Galatina, Italy, presented First, We Bite: Icons, Offerings and the Feminine in Ritual, her joint exhibition with Vilmantas Marcinkevičius, curated by Neringa J. Miller. She is a member of the Lithuanian Artists’ Association and the Lithuanian Design Forum, and divides her time between Lithuania and Galatina, in southern Italy.

Commissions. Through SAVAS Art House, Indra creates commissioned furniture, textile works and complete interior concepts for private residences, estates and hospitality projects in Miami and beyond, each one made by hand for its space.

Patrice Bertin

Born in France, Patrice Bertin trained at the Académie Charpentier and the École Nationale Supérieure des Arts Décoratifs (ENSAD).

He began his career in the conservation and restoration of art. In the 1970s he brought his private conservation practice to New York, where he worked extensively with Old Master, Impressionist and contemporary paintings, including important works associated with Galerie Wildenstein and distinguished private collections.

Years of studying paintings through the eyes of a conservator shaped his own artistic language. His intimate knowledge of pigment, surface, light and structure became the foundation for his painting, as he moved from preserving the vision of others to expressing his own.

Bertin’s paintings explore the tension between color and form, movement and stillness, intuition and structure. Geometric compositions of vertical and horizontal elements create a delicate equilibrium, in which color becomes a means of emotion, rhythm and discovery.

His work is presented to collectors through SAVAS Art House.

Hal Trager

Born in the Bronx and raised on Long Island, Hal Trager has built his art on seeing the world differently.

At two years old, an accident left him with only 35% of the vision in his left eye. For years he believed he saw the world as everyone else did. At nine, sitting with his father in New York City, he began to understand how different his perspective was. Asked to consider the form of a skyscraper, he saw more than a flat surface. He saw depth, dimension and movement. He picked up a pencil and drew what he saw, beginning a lifelong exploration of perspective and perception.

Today Trager works primarily with plastic matte film, creating intricate hand-drawn compositions that challenge the eye and invite the viewer to experience reality from another perspective. His work embodies the essence of Op Art: transforming the ordinary into the extraordinary and making the unseen seen.

His work is presented to collectors through SAVAS Art House.

Natalia Jona

“Shapes dressed in feelings, feelings dressed in shapes” is how Natalia Jona’s work is often described. A London-based artist, she trained at the Władysław Strzemiński Academy of Fine Arts in Łódź, Poland, and later studied at Sotheby’s Institute of Art in New York.

Her paintings are abstractions of the human body, of landscapes and of the literature she loves, rendering natural forms in ways that are simple yet intensely moving. She works in ink with acrylic or oil on fine, nearly textureless unprimed linen, often leaving it exposed as the background. Patterns of small, repeated ink pen strokes give her compositions a hypnotic sense of movement.

Having lived and worked in many countries, she names her years in Japan as a major influence on her vision, alongside Matisse, Miró, Picabia, O’Keeffe, Kandinsky, Strzemiński and Hollowell.

Her work has been presented during Art Basel Miami in 2022, with live painting for the Ferrari Art Car Project; at Zona Maco in Mexico City; in solo exhibitions at Warsaw Gallery Weekend in 2020 and 2021; and in solo and group exhibitions in New York, Tokyo, Kyoto, Milan and Tuscany. She has been featured in Harper’s Bazaar UK, Vogue Polska and L’Officiel Hommes, and was named one of Larry’s List Top 5 Winners. Her paintings are held in private collections across Europe and the United States, including Miami.

Her work is presented to collectors through SAVAS Art House.

Juan Fernando Cobo Agudelo

Santiago de Cali, 1959 – Madrid, 2024

Juan Fernando Cobo Agudelo was a Colombian painter, sculptor and cultural promoter, remembered as el cazador de la luz, the hunter of light. He carried the light of the Colombian tropics into a body of work that resists easy classification.

Over more than four decades, working in studios on three continents, he painted with equal mastery in oil, acrylic and pastel, moving between the figurative and the abstract without losing his singular vision. He trained in Cali, Madrid and New York.

His work is presented to collectors through SAVAS Art House, in collaboration with his family.

Santiago Betancur

Santiago Betancur, born in 1967, is a Colombian Master of Fine Arts based in Miami. Inspired by the horse as one of the most noble and emblematic figures in history, his work explores the profound relationship between humans and animals.

He reinterprets classical imagery in a distinctive Neo-Renaissance style that combines abstraction, rich textures and contemporary expression. He has exhibited in galleries and museums in France, Poland, Spain, Colombia and Miami.

His work is presented to collectors through SAVAS Art House.

About SAVAS Art House

Artists. Exhibitions. Collectors.

SAVAS Art House is an independent art house based in Miami. We represent and work with established international artists, and we bring their work to the places where people live, stay and gather: private residences, hotels, members’ clubs and cultural events.

We have no permanent gallery. Each exhibition is curated for its setting. At EPIC Residences in Miami, twenty-four works by Patrice Bertin and Hal Trager hang in the lobby and corridors, and each label carries a QR code that opens the work, its story and its price.

Our artists include Vilmantas Marcinkevičius, whose painting is held in the European Parliament collection, and the estate of the Colombian painter Juan Fernando Cobo Agudelo, el cazador de la luz.

For collectors, we arrange private viewings and advise on acquisitions and collections. For developers, interior designers, architects and hotels, we select and place art for properties, and commission work made for a specific space.

With representatives in the United Kingdom and across Europe, growing connections in the Middle East and Asia, and a network of artists and partners reaching toward every continent, SAVAS brings an international eye to Miami, and Miami to an international audience.
